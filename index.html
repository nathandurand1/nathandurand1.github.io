Humanitarian design

Hand Crank Grain Mill

Jan. 2026 – May 2026 · Iowa State University

A manually powered grain mill for off-grid rural communities in Nigeria, where roughly 40% of people lack reliable electricity and many millers still rely on slow stone grinding.

  • Led the full design process. I took the mill from problem definition to a finished prototype, setting targets of under $50 and under 15 lbs so it is affordable for small-scale millers and fits the limited storage space they have.
  • Validated the design with engineering analysis. I used QFD to turn user needs into design requirements and DFMEA to find and reduce failure risks, then built three mathematical models to confirm performance: 66.61 lbf·in crank torque, 376.99 rad/min, and a 3 lb/min milling rate.
  • Chose materials for the real user. The mill is built mainly from plywood because wood is available and easy to repair in rural Nigeria.
  • Built it by hand. I fabricated an 8-subassembly plywood prototype with a table saw, miter saw, and drill press, going from initial sketch to physical build, then presented it at the Iowa State University Design Expo.

Accessibility · Human-centered design

Assistive Bowling Device

Sep. 2023 – May 2024

A switch-activated mechanical device that lets people with motor impairments bowl independently. It was donated to a local special-needs school serving wheelchair-dependent children.

  • Designed for independence. The device is switch-activated, so a child with a motor impairment can release the ball and bowl on their own instead of relying on someone else to push it.
  • Modeled and 3D-printed the hardware. I designed the release mechanism and housing in Autodesk Inventor and 3D-printed the parts, mounting the device on a bowling ramp.
  • Iterated with real users. I gathered feedback from the people using it and refined the design for usability, accessibility, and performance.
  • Donated to the community. The finished device was donated to a local special-needs school serving wheelchair-dependent children.

Product design · Additive manufacturing

The Perfect Shoe: 3D-Printed Basketball Shoe

Design and 3D printing project

I designed a low-top basketball shoe from scratch, researching the design choices behind popular performance shoes, then modeled it in CAD and 3D-printed the parts. Footwear professionals from Nike and Adidas served as mentors.

  • Worked with industry engineers. I collaborated with Nike and Adidas engineers to design and 3D-print a basketball shoe, prioritizing athlete safety, comfort, and performance.
  • Modeled complex surfaces in Fusion 360. A shoe has almost no simple geometry, so I modeled its curved, non-geometric surfaces and fine details in Fusion 360 and iterated through additive manufacturing prototypes.
  • Researched materials and performance features. I evaluated footwear materials for durability, flexibility, and cushioning: Filon and TPU mesh for a light, durable upper; a drop-in midsole for court feel; herringbone traction for grip with minimal dust pickup; and a full-length Zoom Strobel unit with a carbon fiber plate for energy return and ankle stability.
  • Designed for comfort. A sock-like collar and ankle padding keep the fit secure without sacrificing comfort.

Product design · CAD

Hot Glue Gun

Mechanical design project

I designed a working hot glue gun in SolidWorks as a full multi-part assembly, not just a single shape. Every component had to be modeled on its own, then fit together so the gun could actually move the way a real one does.

  • Modeled many individual parts. The gun is made up of separate components, including the body, nozzle, heating element housing with a pressure-fitted door, trigger, linkage, and springs, each with its own dimensioned engineering drawing.
  • Built a moving assembly with mates. Getting all of these parts to assemble correctly took an in-depth understanding of SolidWorks assembly mates. I used them to locate each part and constrain only the right degrees of freedom, so the trigger can pivot and the linkage and springs move with it instead of everything being locked in place.
  • Designed to real requirements. The gun had to heat the sticks, dispense from the tip, hold a stable temperature, and not drip. It also had to be lightweight, quick to load, easy to clean, and comfortable to grip, with a viewing window for the stick and non-slip feet so it doesn't tip.
